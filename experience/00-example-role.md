## 1. Context and scope

Northfield Distribution Partners ran a regional distribution center serving independent hardware
retailers. The candidate joined as a picker, was promoted to shift lead after roughly a year, and
from then on coordinated one of the site's four outbound shifts under a rotating operations-manager
structure. `[attested]`

## 2. Systems owned

Owned the outbound shift's pick-pack-ship schedule and its handoff to the loading dock, including
exception handling when an order's pick queue ran behind. Did not own inbound receiving, the site's
staffing budget, or the scheduling software's configuration outside the shift's own queue rules —
those sat with the operations manager. `[attested]`

## 3. Projects and problems

### 3.1 The mis-pick reduction

A quarterly ops review flagged the shift's mis-pick rate as the highest of the site's four shifts.
The existing process relied on pickers self-checking against a paper pick list, with no independent
verification step before an order reached packing. The candidate proposed and piloted a two-person
verification step for high-value orders only, to avoid slowing the whole queue, and tracked the
shift's mis-pick rate weekly for the following quarter using the scheduling software's existing
exception log rather than building any new tooling. Over that quarter the shift's mis-pick rate fell
from 4.1% to 1.6% of orders. `[evidence]` — sourced from the site's quarterly ops report, which the
candidate has an archived copy of; the other three shifts' rates over the same period are not part of
that report and are not claimed here.

### 3.2 Peak-season onboarding

Each of the two peak seasons the candidate worked, the shift temporarily added several seasonal
pickers with no warehouse experience. The candidate wrote a one-page pick-path reference sheet
covering the shift's five most error-prone bin locations and walked each seasonal hire through it on
their first shift. `[attested]` — no record survives of whether this measurably changed the seasonal
hires' error rate; the candidate believes it helped but never tracked it, which is why it is recorded
here as a practice, not a metric.

## 4. Technology

- **EXPOSURE** — the site's inventory-forecasting system one tier up from the scheduling module;
